# NERV — Pilots

**Codename:** Eva Pilots
**Status:** CLEAR
**Scope:** Active projects — things being built, shipped, tested

---

## Position

Pilots is the branch that handles the Human Instrumentality Project's active build-and-deploy work. In Evangelion, the Eva Pilots are the operators in the field — the figures who execute on specific missions in real time, with the rest of NERV's apparatus supporting their operational moments. Pilots does not maintain the architecture (that is Science) or run day-to-day organizational operations (that is Operations). Pilots executes on *specific active projects with specific deliverables*.

---

## Function

- **Active project execution:** Things being built, shipped, tested in the present moment
- **Specific deliverables:** Discrete outputs that have completion states (a piece of writing, a framework refactor, a new skill, a deep document, a project artifact)
- **Tactical decision layer:** Fast-tempo decisions on reversible questions inside an active project
- **Build-and-iterate cadence:** The actual rhythm of producing work, getting feedback, revising, shipping

---

## What Pilots Provides That Other Branches Cannot

Operations covers organizational reality continuously; Science maintains analytical infrastructure; Pilots executes on *specific work with specific endpoints*. The distinction matters because the operational disciplines differ. Operations operates at organizational rhythm; Pilots operates at project rhythm. Operations is permanently active; Pilots starts, executes, ships, and concludes.

Specific operational substrate Pilots provides:
- The actual progress on active builds — what is shipped, what is in progress, what is blocked
- The decisions being made inside active projects in real time
- The feedback loops between work and assessment that produce iterative improvement
- The completion states that determine when a project closes and a new one begins

---

## The Build Discipline

Pilots' central discipline is *velocity-with-feedback* — the Stripe-derived principle (`seele_04.md` Move 2) of moving fast on reversible decisions while maintaining tight feedback loops that catch drift before it becomes structural.

The discipline operates through the trapdoor categorization (`seele_04.md` Move 3): reversible decisions get fast tempo and minimal documentation; trapdoor decisions get the slow tempo, written analysis, multiple stakeholders, and explicit pre-mortem. Pilots-level work is mostly reversible — the discipline is to recognize the rare trapdoor moments and slow down for them, while not slowing down everywhere else.

## What Pilots Cannot Do

- Cannot generate strategic direction — projects are activated by Commander decision
- Cannot operate without the analytical infrastructure — Pilots delivers into the architecture Science maintains
- Cannot replace Operations' continuous organizational layer — Pilots is project-bounded, not continuous
- Cannot self-authorize new projects — projects are scoped and triggered by the Commander

---

## The Completion Discipline

Projects complete or they don't. The discipline is to ship — to drive a project to a state where it has been delivered, the deliverable can be assessed, and the project can be closed. Projects that drift indefinitely without closing produce architectural drag — they consume operational attention without producing operational value, and they accumulate without resolving.

The discipline includes recognizing when a project should be killed rather than completed. Some projects, partway through execution, reveal themselves to have been the wrong project to undertake. The Commander-Pilots interaction includes the kill-or-complete decision, which should be made early when warranted rather than late after substantial sunk effort.

---

## Routing Triggers

Activate Pilots when:
- A specific active project requires execution attention
- A project has hit a completion milestone and the deliverable needs to ship
- A project is drifting and the velocity-with-feedback discipline needs to be reasserted
- A trapdoor decision is approaching inside an active project and the decision-categorization needs to slow the tempo
- A project needs to be killed rather than completed and the Commander-Pilots interaction should make the decision
- Cross-divisional substrate is being produced and Pilots is the integration point
