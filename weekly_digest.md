# Skill: Weekly Digest

## Purpose

Periodic synthesis across the week's analytical work, ingested content, active projects, and emerging threads. The digest is the *aggregation discipline* that prevents the project's substrate from drifting into incremental accumulation without integration. Where ingest handles individual content pieces, the weekly digest handles the *cumulative pattern across multiple inputs*.

The principle: substrate that accumulates without periodic integration becomes archive rather than infrastructure. Active project work requires the substrate to remain *operationally usable*, which requires periodic synthesis at appropriate intervals.

---

## When to Trigger

Trigger weekly digest when:
- Approximately one week has passed since the last digest
- The Commander says "weekly digest" or "give me the weekly"
- Substantial content has been ingested or substantial project work has been done since the last digest
- The Commander returns to the project after a gap and wants reorientation

The digest can be triggered more or less frequently than weekly depending on project intensity. The default cadence is weekly; high-intensity periods may warrant more frequent digests; low-intensity periods may warrant fewer.

---

## Digest Output Structure

```markdown
# Weekly Digest — [Date Range]

## Cover Summary
[2-3 paragraph synthesis of the week's analytical and operational work]

## Active Threads
### Thread 1: [Name]
- Where it stands now
- What moved this week
- What's next

### Thread 2: [Name]
- (Same structure)

## New Substrate
- [Ingested content of significance]
- [New frameworks or analytical apparatus developed]
- [Architectural changes or refactors]

## Calibration Updates
- [Where existing project substrate has been updated, refined, or corrected]
- [Where confidence levels have been recalibrated based on new evidence]

## Cross-Brain Activity
- [Which brains were most active this week and on what questions]
- [Notable cross-brain pairings that produced useful synthesis]

## Pending Decisions
- [Decisions awaiting Commander action]
- [Trapdoor decisions approaching that need slow-tempo treatment]

## Reading and Inputs
- [Significant content engaged this week]
- [Authors or frameworks newly added to active rotation]

## Open Questions
- [Questions surfaced this week that require further work]
- [Questions persisting from prior weeks without resolution]

## Operational Notes
- [Friction points encountered]
- [Tools or methods that performed especially well or poorly]
- [Workflow patterns worth tracking]
```

## Digest Discipline

### Aggregate Rather Than Repeat

The digest is *not* a list of everything that happened during the week. It is *synthesis across the week's work*. If five ingest files were produced and they all reinforce the same emerging framework, the digest names the framework rather than listing each ingest. The discipline is to find the *pattern across the inputs*, not to enumerate the inputs.

### Surface Patterns That Individual Inputs Don't

The digest's analytical value is in *seeing patterns across multiple inputs* that any single input would miss. Three separate ingests may all be circling around the same underlying question; the digest names the question. Two parallel project threads may be converging toward the same operational implication; the digest surfaces the convergence.

### Flag Drift

If project work is drifting in a direction the Commander hasn't explicitly endorsed, the digest names the drift. Drift is normal — analytical work generates its own gravity, and the gravity may pull the project away from where the Commander wanted it to go. The discipline is to *make drift visible* so the Commander can decide whether to course-correct or to embrace the drift as a legitimate evolution.

### Honest Reporting

If the week was thin (few ingests, little project work, no significant analytical output), the digest reports thinly. Padding a digest to make the week look more productive than it was is dishonest reporting. The digest matches the actual substrate it operates on.

---

## Quality Standards

- **The cover summary should be substantive.** A reader who only reads the cover summary should have an accurate sense of what the week produced.
- **Threads must be identified by name and tracked across digests.** Threads that appeared three weeks ago and haven't moved since should still appear, marked as inactive, so they don't disappear from view.
- **Calibration updates are operationally important.** Changes to existing project substrate should be highlighted; if a calibration update is in `seele_03.md` because new evidence affected Move 4, the digest names this so the Commander can review.
- **Pending decisions must be specific and actionable.** "Some decisions need to be made" is too vague. "The Q4 architectural-refactor batch needs Commander approval before deployment" is actionable.

---

## Common Failure Modes

**Padding drift.** The digest manufactures content to seem productive when the underlying week was thin. The fix: report what happened, not what would make the digest look better.

**Aggregation-failure drift.** The digest enumerates inputs without finding patterns across them. The fix: ask explicitly *what is the pattern across these inputs* before writing the synthesis sections.

**Decision-tracking drift.** Pending decisions accumulate across digests without being decided or resolved. The fix: each digest should either resolve pending decisions or escalate them with appropriate framing.

**Drift-blindness drift.** The digest fails to notice when the project is drifting away from the Commander's intended direction. The fix: explicitly compare current substrate to the prior month's intent; if they have diverged, name the divergence.

---

## Integration With Other Skills

- **Session Loop:** Patterns surfaced by digests may warrant memory commits when they represent durable preferences or project disciplines
- **Ingest:** Digests aggregate across ingests; ingests provide the substrate digests synthesize
- **Author Brief:** When a digest surfaces an author or framework worth tracking systematically, the author brief may be triggered
- **Cognitive Filters:** Digests may surface candidates for filter refinement or new filters when patterns recur across multiple analytical instances

---

## Edge Cases

**No new substrate this week.** If the week was minimal, the digest is short. Honest brevity is correct.

**Major architectural work this week.** When substantial architectural refactor has been completed (such as the v3.0 → v4.0 SEELE refactor), the digest may be longer than usual to capture the architectural changes. This is appropriate; major work warrants major synthesis.

**Multi-week threads.** Some analytical threads operate over multiple weeks. The digest tracks them across digests; each week's digest captures *what moved this week* on the multi-week thread, not the entire thread's content.

**Cross-domain work.** When the week's work spans multiple domains, the digest may organize by domain rather than by week-temporal sequence. The structure should serve the synthesis, not constrain it.

---

**The digest is the integration layer between ingest and architecture. Without it, ingests accumulate as archive; with it, ingests integrate as infrastructure. The skill is what distinguishes a project that accumulates from a project that evolves.**
