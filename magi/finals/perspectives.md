# Perspectives — where credible sources disagree

Doctrine is not fact. Where good sources conflict, this file holds the conflict open rather than
flattening it into a false consensus — and says which way the verified numbers lean.

The last section holds **my own analytical positions**, clearly separated. Those are reasoning from
`numbers.md`, not received wisdom, and should be held more loosely than either.

**What's actually agreed:** every winning composition contains at least one Medium and at least one
Heavy. Medium and Heavy are both S-tier `[V]`. Nobody credible recommends stacking Light.

**The real disagreement** is whether the third slot is a second Heavy (raw durability and space
control) or a Light (off-angles, glitch utility, tempo). MHH is described as more forgiving; LMH as
higher-ceiling and harder.

`[I]` Note the sources aren't actually contradicting each other so much as answering different
questions — "what wins tournaments with a coordinated team" versus "what is robust in solo queue."
Those have different answers, and conflating them is how comp advice gets confusing.

# My own positions

Reasoning from the verified numbers rather than from any source. Marked separately because they
carry less authority than either the wiki or the competitive scene — they are arguments, and they
should lose to evidence.

## A. Public doctrine badly under-weights the coin economy

Across every competitive guide surveyed, the respawn-coin system gets almost no strategic treatment.
The Defibrillator is recommended for tempo and convenience; almost nobody frames it as **the team's
primary resource-conservation instrument**, which — given that coins are spent on respawn and not
revive `[V]` — is what it actually is.

If that's genuinely under-discussed rather than just under-documented, it's an available edge. A
team that budgets coins deliberately is playing a resource game its opponents aren't tracking.

## B. Double Jeopardy is nearly absent from tactical discussion

A mechanic that can cost **50% of total match cash** `[V]` barely appears in the strategy material.
Either the situation is rarer than it looks, or it's a widely-made unforced error. Both readings
argue for the same discipline: never feed a second box into a station you can't hold.

## C. "Keep one player alive" should be a role, not an accident

The competitive sources list late wipes as a mistake to avoid `[V]`. That framing is passive.
Given that a steal is a fixed 7 seconds `[V]`, a single living body near the terminal in the final
window is worth more than any amount of damage dealt elsewhere. **Assign it before the fight
starts** — one player is designated survivor in the last 30 seconds, with permission to disengage.

## D. The sniper's competitive role is Opener, not Anchor

The most useful synthesis in this file for the Commander specifically.

The role framework in `pro_play.md` §3 defines three defensive jobs. Two of them are hostile to a
bolt-action sniper: **Anchor** demands close-range denial and survivability, **Support** demands
revive-range proximity and sustain. But **Opener** — outer angle or upper floor, spots early, breaks
the first entry, calls the push — maps almost exactly onto what a sniper is good at.

That reframes the "sniper isn't meta" problem `[V]`. The weapon isn't bad; it's *role-constrained*.
A sniper Light playing Opener is doing a job the team needs. A sniper Light trying to Anchor a
cashout is playing the weapon in the one role it cannot fill.

Practical consequence: if the team has no Medium anchoring and no Heavy holding space, the sniper's
weakness becomes structural rather than personal. **The SR-84 needs a comp that covers for it.**

## E. Fighting is under-priced by this substrate's own early framing, and over-priced by mid-rank instinct

Both errors exist. Eliminations pay $500 `[V]` — real money, and in ranked they drain coins too.
But a station pot is $8,000+ `[V]`. The resolution isn't "fight more" or "fight less" — it's
**proximity**. A kill taken from your holding angle is free money; the identical kill twenty meters
away is how you lose the pot. The variable that matters is not whether to fight but *where*.
