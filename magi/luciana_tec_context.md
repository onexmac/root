## ATI — The Chosen Path

**Full name:** Licenciatura en Administración de Tecnología de Información (TEC)
**Plan:** 2051 (updated 2020, currently active for new students)
**Location:** Campus Tecnológico Central, Cartago — only campus offering this carrera
**Acreditación:** SINAES (national, reaccredited 2021); position is dual under both Escuela de Computación and Escuela de Administración de Empresas (responds directly to vicerrectoría de Docencia)

### What the plan 2051 covers

The curriculum reform that came into effect in 2020 reorganized the carrera around modern enterprise IT competencies:

- **Ciberseguridad** — security as a core area, not an elective
- **Analítica empresarial** — data science applied to business decision-making
- **Arquitectura empresarial** — enterprise systems architecture as a discipline
- **Calidad en sistemas de información** — quality assurance and systems integrity
- **Investigación en sistemas de información** — research methodology in IS
- **Programación orientada a objetos** — applied programming
- **Habilidades estratégicas** — communication (oral and written), applied math
- **Gestión de negocios** — business administration as integrated layer

The reform consulted employers, alumni, faculty, and similar programs in América, Europe, and Asia before approval. The curriculum is genuinely current — not 2010-era IT taught in 2026.

### What ATI graduates do

The carrera positions itself explicitly as the "intérprete-negociador" between business needs and technology solutions — a role that requires both technical fluency and business understanding. Employers in conversation with the program include Microsoft, Intel, IBM, and Deloitte. Approximately 200+ graduates as of 2021; the program has had sustained growth since first graduation in 2012.

Career roles for ATI graduates typically include:
- Enterprise architecture consultant
- Information systems strategy lead
- Business analyst (technical)
- IT project manager
- Security and compliance lead
- Cross-functional integrator between IT and business units

### Why ATI fits Luciana

Three reasons surfaced in the earlier evaluation:

**One.** ATI is genuinely modern. The plan 2051 reform addressed the criticism that traditional IT carreras taught yesterday's tools. Luciana is starting in 2026; she'll graduate around 2030-2031. The plan is current enough to remain relevant through that timeline, with cybersecurity and analytics as foundational layers rather than electives.

**Two.** ATI sits at the intersection of business and technology — exactly the bilingual register that pairs with behavioral economics at the maestría level. A pure CS/Ingeniería en Computación path would over-index on technical depth at the cost of the business-decision register. A pure Administración de Empresas would under-index on technical fluency. ATI is the integrating discipline.

**Three.** Luciana's profile (smart, brave, bold, systematic) maps to the program's stated student profile: pensamiento abstracto, pensamiento crítico, capacidad comunicativa, habilidades de liderazgo, sensibilidad social. The fit is structural, not just convenient.

## Alternatives Considered

### Administración de Empresas (TEC)

Traditional business administration. Strong program with international accreditation, but the technical layer is light — the carrera doesn't build the systems-and-data fluency that the modern economy increasingly demands of senior business operators.

**Why it was passed over:** Luciana's analytical disposition warrants the technical depth. Admin Empresas would underutilize her math and abstract-pensamiento capacity. Behavioral economics at the maestría level pairs better with quantitative undergraduate training than with a generalist business degree.

### Ingeniería en Producción Industrial (TEC)

Internationally accredited (Canadian Engineering Accreditation Board / CEAB). Strong technical program covering manufacturing systems, logistics, supply chain, productivity engineering. Costa Rica has significant demand for this profile due to multinational operations (medical devices, services, high-tech manufacturing).

**Why it was passed over:** The carrera is excellent for someone going into industrial operations, but the curriculum is heavily oriented toward physical-process engineering (manufacturing, logistics, materials). The information-systems layer is present but secondary. ATI inverts that emphasis — information systems as primary, business operations as integrated layer. For someone whose downstream interest is decision-making and behavior (behavioral economics), ATI is the closer fit.

### Other paths not deeply evaluated

- **Ingeniería en Computación (TEC)** — too technical-deep for the behavioral econ pairing
- **UCR or other public-university IT programs** — TEC's program is more current and more business-integrated
- **Private universities (ULACIT, ULatina, etc.)** — quality variance and credentialing concerns relative to public TEC

The shortlist was always ATI / Admin Empresas / Ing. Industrial because those three sit closest to the bilingual technical-business register that the behavioral econ pairing required.

## The Post-Licenciatura Plan

The articulated plan: **maestría in behavioral economics, Spain.**

### Why this combination works

ATI builds the *substrate*: data fluency, systems thinking, business operations literacy. Behavioral economics at the maestría level builds the *interpretive layer*: how human decisions actually work, where rational-actor models fail, how to design systems that account for cognitive biases.

The pairing is non-obvious but structurally sound. ATI graduates typically go into IT operations, consulting, or business analysis. The behavioral econ overlay opens a different lane: product decision-making, behavioral design, decision-architecture roles in fintech, healthtech, government, or research institutions. The combination is rarer in Costa Rica's market, which is a feature — Luciana would not be one of many.

### What's still to verify (Spain side)

The Spain maestría target is articulated but the specific program hasn't been pinned down. Programs worth investigating when the time approaches (around year 4 of the licenciatura):

- **UPF Barcelona** — strong behavioral economics tradition; multiple master's programs at the intersection of economics and psychology
- **Universidad Carlos III de Madrid** — Master in Economics with behavioral track; strong research orientation
- **IE Business School (Madrid)** — Master in Behavioral Science applied to business; more practitioner-oriented
- **Universidad de Granada** — programs in psychology and decision sciences with economic applications
- **Universidad Autónoma de Barcelona** — Master in Applied Economics with behavioral components

This list is research-direction, not recommendation. Each program has different strengths (academic vs. applied, economics-first vs. psychology-first, English vs. Spanish track) and the right choice depends on what Luciana's interests have crystallized into by year 4-5 of the licenciatura.

**Cost and logistics not yet evaluated:** tuition ranges, scholarship availability, EU vs. non-EU tuition tiers, residency requirements, length of program (1 vs. 2 years), employment-during-study allowances.

## Honest Gaps

Three things this file does not establish, and shouldn't pretend to:

**One.** This file captures the analytical reasoning at the time of decision. It does not capture Luciana's interior experience of the choice — what she's excited about, what she's worried about, what would make her change paths. That belongs in conversation with her, not in this substrate.

**Two.** TEC's plan 2051 is current as of 2020. Curriculum reforms happen on cycles of 5-10 years; the next reform may be relevant by Luciana's graduation. Worth re-verifying program currency at year 3 of the licenciatura.

**Three.** The behavioral economics market — both academic and professional — is evolving. The pairing's strength in 2026 may not be identical to its strength in 2032 when Luciana finishes the maestría. The combination still hedges well across multiple futures (data + decision-architecture is robust to AI disruption in ways that pure technical or pure business paths aren't), but the specific market positioning will shift and should be reassessed near maestría completion.
