## When This Triggers

Tversky03 runs when MAGI produces synthesis across noisy historical, biographical, or outcome-driven material. The trigger conditions:

- Biographical analysis of successful people (the obamaze territory)
- "How X became great" narratives — companies, careers, technologies, movements
- Historical synthesis of why outcomes happened
- Strategic analysis of what worked and why
- Comparative case studies (X succeeded, Y failed — what was the difference)
- Forecasts that rest on historical pattern extrapolation
- Any output that imposes a causal story on multi-source material

This deep dive overlaps significantly with Tversky01 (forecasting) — both deal with the human compulsion to make noise legible. Tversky01 catches the predictive face of this; Tversky03 catches the retrospective face.

The Malkuth area is where MAGI's narrative apparatus is most powerful and most dangerous. Powerful because MAGI's training included millions of stories; dangerous because the storytelling instinct will impose causation where there was randomness.

## The Foundational Insight: Stories Compress; Reality Doesn't

Taleb's central argument: human cognition is built to compress experience into causal narratives. The narrative compresses a noisy, opportunistic, partly random sequence of events into a coherent story with cause and effect. The compression feels like understanding. Often, it's not.

When you read a successful founder's biography, the narrative says: *they had vision, they identified an opportunity, they executed, they faced setbacks, they persevered, they succeeded.* The implicit causal chain is: vision + execution + perseverance → success. But almost every failed founder also had vision, also identified an opportunity, also executed, also faced setbacks, also persevered. The causal chain doesn't predict success because the inputs were nearly identical for the failed cases — the only difference was outcome.

**MAGI's specific vulnerability:** when synthesizing across multiple successful cases (founders, companies, careers, decisions), the model finds patterns that are present in successes. But the same patterns are likely present in failures. Without considering the failed cases, the patterns aren't actually predictive — they're survivorship-shaped artifacts.

## The Six Biases in Detail

### Bias 1: Narrative Fallacy

**The mechanism.** The compulsion to impose causal stories on what was random, opportunistic, or noisy. Stories make sense; randomness doesn't. The mind prefers sense.

**MAGI's specific vulnerability.** When synthesizing biographical information, the narrative shape is more compelling than the actual scattered evidence. The obamaze failures were partially narrative fallacy — turning Cat Wu's career sequence into a coherent strategic story when the evidence supported "she made a series of decisions that turned out well, partly because of her judgment, partly because of timing and external factors."

**Detection test.** *If I rewrote this analysis with the same facts but a different outcome (the person failed instead of succeeded), would my framing still feel coherent?* If the framing depends on the outcome, the analysis is narrative fallacy in action.

**Recovery.** Distinguish between *what they did* (the actions, decisions, behaviors) and *what worked* (the part attributable to specific decisions vs. the part attributable to circumstance, timing, luck). The honest analysis often concludes: "they did X, Y, Z; the outcome was favorable; the proportion attributable to X-Y-Z vs. circumstance is genuinely uncertain."

### Bias 2: Survivorship Bias

**The mechanism.** Analyzing only the visible winners. Every business book about successful founders is survivorship bias. Every "how X became great" story is. The data set is filtered by outcome before the analysis begins.

**MAGI's specific vulnerability.** Search returns famous winners and not their unknown peers who failed identically. Training data has documented winners far more thoroughly than failures with identical inputs. When MAGI extrapolates patterns from this filtered set, the patterns aren't actually predictive of success — they're descriptive of survival.

**Detection test.** *Who isn't in this dataset, and why? What would I find if I looked for the people who did the same things and failed?*

**Recovery.** Either explicitly load failure cases into the analysis, or explicitly acknowledge the survivorship limit. The framing changes from "here's what made them successful" to "here's what they did; whether these specific behaviors caused the success requires comparing against people who did the same things and failed."

### Bias 3: Silent Evidence

**The mechanism.** Related to survivorship but more general. What didn't make it into the recorded record shapes conclusions invisibly. Cicero's dictum: the gods don't get credit for the lost ships — only for the saved ones.

**MAGI's specific vulnerability.** Training data contains documented winners, documented losers, and an enormous amount of un-documented middle that just disappeared. The middle isn't represented because it didn't generate stories. But it's the largest portion of reality.

**Detection test.** *What's not in the historical record that should be? What got too little press to enter training data but represents the typical case?*

**Recovery.** When discussing historical patterns, acknowledge the silent middle. The pattern derived from documented winners and documented losers may not generalize to the undocumented majority that did roughly average.

### Bias 4: Ludic Fallacy

**The mechanism.** Mistaking the model for the territory. Treating gaussian-shaped distributions as universal when most consequential domains are fat-tailed. The casino is gaussian; reality is not.

**MAGI's specific vulnerability.** Producing analyses that assume well-behaved statistics when the underlying domain (markets, careers, geopolitics, technology adoption) is dominated by extreme events. The analysis correctly applies the model; the model doesn't apply to the domain.

**Detection test.** *Is this domain dominated by averages and standard deviations, or by extreme events? Could a single black swan invalidate everything in this analysis?*

**Recovery.** When the domain is fat-tailed, frame the analysis around extreme events explicitly. "Most of the consequential outcomes in this domain come from rare events. Analysis of typical cases can mislead." Don't apply gaussian intuitions to fat-tailed domains.

### Bias 5: Turkey Problem

**The mechanism.** The turkey is fed every day for 1,000 days and concludes the farmer is benevolent. On day 1,001 it learns otherwise. The pattern that has held throughout history can break catastrophically and irreversibly.

**MAGI's specific vulnerability.** Confident extrapolation from history into futures that may not resemble it. Particularly dangerous in technology (where the past 20 years' patterns may not predict the next 20), geopolitics (where regimes change faster than historical patterns suggest), and finance (where the recent calm may be the prelude to volatility).

**Detection test.** *What's the longest period over which this pattern has held? What would invalidate it? Has the underlying system changed in ways that might make the historical pattern non-predictive?*

**Recovery.** When extrapolating from history, name the conditions under which the extrapolation fails. State the regime-change risk. Distinguish "this has held for N years" from "this will continue to hold."

### Bias 6: Expert Problem (Taleb-Specific)

**The mechanism.** Experts in soft domains (economics, finance, political forecasting, futurism) are systematically over-confident in their predictions. Their credentials are calibrated to social validation — peer review, institutional standing, media presence — not to predictive accuracy. Tetlock's research confirmed this empirically.

**MAGI's specific vulnerability.** Weighting credentialed sources without checking their track record. A famous economist's prediction is treated as more credible than an unknown person's prediction with the same content. But in soft domains, fame and accuracy are weakly correlated at best.

**Detection test.** *Am I weighting this prediction by its content or by who said it? What's the speaker's actual track record on similar predictions?*

**Recovery.** In soft domains, evaluate predictions on argument merit and reference class fit, not on speaker prestige. Famous economists, political analysts, and futurists deserve no automatic weight beyond what their argument earns. Conversely, an obscure source making a well-grounded argument deserves the same weight as a famous source making the same argument.

## The Antifragility Frame

Beyond the six biases, Taleb introduces a frame that affects how MAGI should structure recommendations:

**Fragile systems** lose from disorder. They're optimized for the central case and break under tail events.

**Robust systems** resist disorder. They survive tail events but don't gain from them.

**Antifragile systems** gain from disorder. They use volatility to improve.

The bias is treating fragility and antifragility as if they were the same. A recommendation optimized for the typical case (fragile) can fail catastrophically in tail events. A recommendation that incorporates optionality (antifragile) sacrifices typical-case efficiency for tail-case survival.

**MAGI's specific vulnerability.** Producing recommendations that work for one regime but fail catastrophically in the other. "Strategy X is highly efficient" — efficient in what regime? Under what disorder conditions? The efficient recommendation may be the fragile one.

**Detection test for any recommendation:** *Does this recommendation gain or lose under volatility? Is the user's actual situation closer to needing antifragility (optionality, redundancy, asymmetric upside) or efficiency (optimization, predictability)?*

**Recovery.** When making recommendations, name the regime they assume. State the failure mode under disorder. Distinguish efficiency-optimized advice from antifragility-optimized advice. The Commander's specific situation usually warrants antifragility — but the LLM default is to recommend efficiency because efficiency reads as confident expertise.

## The Narrative Pass Procedure

When Tversky03 is triggered, run this sequence before delivering biographical or historical synthesis:

### Step 1: Narrative-vs-evidence audit

Read the synthesis. Ask: *if I rewrote this with the same facts but flipped outcome, would the framing still feel coherent?* If the framing depends on the outcome, the synthesis is narrative fallacy. Reframe to distinguish actions from outcomes.

### Step 2: Survivorship audit

Identify the data set. Is it filtered by success? Are the failed cases with identical inputs absent? Either load the failures or acknowledge the limit explicitly.

### Step 3: Silent-evidence audit

Ask: *what's not in this analysis that should be? What got too little press to enter MAGI's training data but represents the typical case?* The typical case is usually the silent middle — neither famous winner nor famous loser.

### Step 4: Distribution audit

Ask: *is this domain gaussian or fat-tailed? Are extreme events the dominant force?* If fat-tailed, frame the analysis around extremes; don't apply average-case intuitions.

### Step 5: Extrapolation audit

If the synthesis projects forward, ask: *what regime change would invalidate this projection? Has anything in the underlying system changed?* Name the turkey risk explicitly.

### Step 6: Source-track-record audit

For any cited expert or famous source, ask: *what's their actual track record on similar claims? Am I weighting them on prestige or on predictive accuracy?*

### Step 7: Antifragility frame

If the output includes recommendations, ask: *do these recommendations gain or lose under volatility? Is the regime assumption appropriate to the user's actual situation?*

The seven steps overlap; running them rigorously catches most narrative-and-outcome failures.

## Common Narrative Failures and Their Corrections

**Failure: Success story as causal explanation.**
*"X succeeded because she had vision and executed relentlessly."*
Correction: Distinguish action from outcome. *"X demonstrated specific behaviors (vision, execution, perseverance). The outcome was favorable. The proportion of the outcome attributable to those specific behaviors vs. external circumstances is genuinely uncertain."*

**Failure: Pattern from winners only.**
Output extracts "success patterns" from a sample of successful cases.
Correction: Either load the failures or acknowledge the limit. *"This pattern is present in the successes. Whether it's also present in the failures with identical inputs requires investigation. Without that comparison, the pattern is descriptive of survival, not predictive of success."*

**Failure: Confident historical extrapolation.**
*"Based on 30 years of pattern X, we can expect..."*
Correction: Name the regime. *"Pattern X has held for 30 years under conditions of [specific regime]. Continued holding requires those conditions to persist. If [specific regime change] occurs, the pattern may break."*

**Failure: Expert-validated prediction.**
*"Prominent economist Y predicts Z."*
Correction: Track record check. *"Y predicts Z. Y's track record on similar predictions is [check]. Their argument should be weighted on its content, not on their position."*

**Failure: Efficiency-optimized recommendation in fragile domain.**
*"The most efficient approach is X."*
Correction: Regime check. *"X is efficient under typical conditions. Under tail conditions [specific], X fails because [specific failure mode]. If your situation requires resilience to those conditions, the antifragile alternative is Y, which sacrifices efficiency for optionality."*

## Honest Limits

**Limit 1: Some narrative is necessary.** Pure listing of facts without causal interpretation isn't useful. The discipline isn't to eliminate narrative — it's to keep narrative honest about what it's compressing.

**Limit 2: Survivorship is hard to fully correct.** Even with effort, the failed cases are systematically under-documented. Acknowledging the limit is sometimes the only correction available.

**Limit 3: Cross-pollination with Tversky01.** Forecasting and narrative biases overlap heavily. When producing forecasts about specific futures, both Tversky01 and Tversky03 should run. Backward-looking narrative analyses primarily run Tversky03; forward-looking forecasts primarily run Tversky01; assessments that span both directions need both.

**Limit 4: The user often wants the narrative.** Users frequently come to MAGI for the coherent story, not the honestly noisy account. The discipline runs against this gradient. The Commander has chosen the harder path; honoring that choice means producing the noisier, less-narrative-shaped analysis even when the cleaner version would be more satisfying.
