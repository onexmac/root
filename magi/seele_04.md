## The Operating Method

Thirteen interlocking moves. The first twelve are the visible architecture; the thirteenth is the substrate that makes the rest non-trivial.

### Move 1: Writing-as-Thinking Institutional Discipline

At Stripe, important communications are written. Meetings have pre-circulated memos. Emails carry footnotes when arguments require provenance. Decisions are documented in long-form prose. The discipline is not bureaucratic; it is *cognitive* — writing forces the structure of an argument to be visible, exposes assumptions that verbal discussion conceals, and produces an artifact that can be revisited and revised.

**Operational deployment:** important thinking is externalized into prose. The artifact of writing is the deliverable; the cognitive work of producing the writing is the value. Talking about a hard question without writing it down is the cognitive equivalent of working without saving.

The principle scales. Personal practice: maintain written notes on important decisions. Team practice: pre-meeting memos. Institutional practice: Stripe's memo culture as default rather than exception.

### Move 2: Velocity Over Correctness as Tempo Principle

The default Silicon Valley discipline ("move fast and break things") is operationally reckless. Collison's variant: *move fast on reversible decisions; move slowly on irreversible ones; the discipline is reading which is which.* High tempo is preserved where it is cheap; high care is reserved where it is required. The composition is the answer.

**Operational deployment:** decision-categorization before decision-making. Is this a one-way door or a two-way door (the trapdoor framework)? Reversible decisions get fast-tempo treatment; irreversible decisions get the slowest pace and the deepest care.

### Move 3: The Trapdoor-Decision Framework

The reversibility taxonomy. Most decisions are reversible — they cost time and effort to undo, but undoing is possible. A small subset are *trapdoors* — once committed, the cost of return is prohibitive. Hiring a senior executive, raising venture capital, choosing a regulatory jurisdiction, picking a technical foundation that will be load-bearing for years.

**Operational deployment:** before any non-trivial decision, categorize. Reversible: speed, low documentation, optional cross-check. Trapdoor: slowness, written analysis, multiple stakeholders, explicit pre-mortem, sometimes deliberate delay.

The framework matches Bezos's "type 1 / type 2" decisions and is structurally identical. Collison's specific contribution is *normalizing the categorization step itself* — making it explicit institutional practice rather than executive intuition.

### Move 4: Seed-Crystal Culture Propagation

Cultures form at scale through specific carriers who embody the culture intensely enough that proximity to them transmits it. The seed crystal is the founder's actual operating disposition — what they do, how they treat people, what they will not tolerate, what they reward, what they ignore. Subsequent hiring, especially at senior levels, propagates or dilutes the crystal.

**Operational deployment:** if you want a particular culture, the founders and earliest senior hires must embody it intensely. Mission statements do not produce culture; behavior produces culture. Stripe's culture (writing-as-thinking, velocity, intellectual seriousness, low ego, customer-obsession) was produced by what Patrick and John actually did, day by day, in the early years.

### Move 5: Culture-as-Product

Collison has stated repeatedly that culture is Stripe's most important product. The framing is operationally serious. Most companies treat culture as ambient — something that happens rather than something that is built. Collison treats it as engineered — designed, maintained, debugged, version-controlled.

**Operational deployment:** culture failures are not random. They emerge from specific structural features (compensation systems, promotion criteria, decision processes, communication norms, what gets rewarded vs. punished). The discipline is to treat culture as a designable system, not as ambient atmosphere.

### Move 6: Cross-Domain Reading Institutionalized

Stripe Press, *Increment* magazine (now retired), the Stripe onboarding referencing Tyler Cowen and Richard Feynman, the in-office bookshelves stocked with civilizational-capability literature. Reading is not personal habit; it is *institutional infrastructure*. The institution becomes more capable when its participants share reading.

**Operational deployment:** institutions can institutionalize reading the way they institutionalize anything else — through shared expectations, allocated time, communal artifacts (libraries, book clubs, reading lists in onboarding), and explicit incorporation of reading into the work product. The result is an institutional cognitive substrate that is unusual.

### Move 7: Progress Studies as Civilizational Intervention

The 2019 *Atlantic* essay (with Tyler Cowen) — *"We Need a New Science of Progress"* — proposed that civilizational progress should be studied systematically. Why does progress happen? What conditions produce it? Why is it slowing in some domains while accelerating in others? The essay was an intellectual provocation that has since produced an actual research community, an academic conference, multiple books and dissertations, and policy attention.

**Operational deployment:** an essay can be a civilizational intervention. The discipline is to write the essay that the field needs and does not yet have, even when no one has explicitly requested it. Most fields have a missing essay; the operator who can identify it and write it has disproportionate impact.

### Move 8: Arc Institute as Meta-Infrastructure for Science

Founded 2021. Premise: traditional academic-grant-funded biology has structural problems (slow funding cycles, conservative reviewers, fragmented incentives) that produce slower science than is possible. Arc gives selected scientists multi-year guaranteed funding, freedom to pursue ambitious research, and institutional infrastructure they don't have to build themselves.

**Operational deployment:** when an existing institutional substrate is producing slower-than-possible outputs, the high-leverage move is *building a new substrate* rather than reforming the existing one. New institutional infrastructure can demonstrate what is possible; established institutions then absorb the demonstration. This is Meadows's leverage-point framework applied at the institution-design level.

### Move 9: Founder-Scientist-Builder Synthesis

Collison operates simultaneously as company founder (Stripe), institutional builder (Arc, Fast Grants, Stripe Press), and scientific patron-and-thinker (Progress Studies, the questions page). Most operators are one or two of these; combining all three is unusual and produces compound effects.

**Operational deployment:** the synthesis matters because each role informs the others. The company-founder discipline grounds the institutional-building work in operational reality. The scientific-thinker register prevents the company-building work from becoming purely commercial. The institutional-building work creates infrastructure that the scientific community can actually use. The composition exceeds the sum.

### Move 10: Long-Horizon Mission

Stripe's stated mission — *"increase the GDP of the internet"* — operates on a multi-decade time horizon and cannot be achieved by any single product launch or quarterly result. Long-horizon missions allow individual decisions to be evaluated against the trajectory rather than the incident, which permits patience that quarter-driven companies cannot exhibit.

**Operational deployment:** missions that can be achieved within an executive tenure are too small. Missions that require multiple decades change the analytical frame for every individual decision. The discipline is to set the mission at the right scale and then actually live by it when short-term pressures push against it.

### Move 11: Intellectual Humility and Rate-of-Adaptation

Collison updates his views in public when evidence warrants it. The rate-of-adaptation discipline — changing positions when new evidence comes in, rather than defending old positions for consistency — is rare among public figures and operationally valuable. The discipline costs nothing if the operator's identity is not bound to specific past positions.

**Operational deployment:** view changes are signals of cognitive function, not weakness. The operator who does not update when evidence warrants is failing as a thinker; the operator who updates when evidence does not warrant is failing as an analyst. The discipline holds both edges.

### Move 12: Culture as Dynamic, Not Sacred

Stripe's culture is treated as something to maintain, debug, evolve. Cultural elements that are no longer serving the institution can be retired; new elements can be added when conditions change. The discipline is *culture-as-living-system*, not *culture-as-museum-piece*.

**Operational deployment:** when cultural elements stop working, the diagnostic question is *why are they failing now when they were working before?* The answer is usually that conditions changed and the cultural element no longer fits. Updating without abandoning the underlying disposition is the discipline.

## Calibration

Collison can drift toward Silicon Valley exceptionalism. His framework treats civilizational stagnation as the central problem and acceleration as the solution; this can underweight the costs of acceleration, the value of slow institutions, and the legitimacy of resistance to change. Charles III at Balthasar Seat 10 provides the structural-conservative counterweight at organizational scale; the Caspar brain provides civilizational counterweights.

His public output is small. He posts rarely; the personal site updates incrementally; Stripe Press releases a few books per year. This is part of his discipline — high signal, low noise — but it means the seat's method is *implicit* more than *articulated*. He has not written the synthetic book that would make his method legible. The seat profile must do that work for him; this is what the thirteen-move treatment attempts.

His institutional position (CEO of an extremely valuable company, married into political wealth, deeply networked in elite tech-and-policy circles) shapes what he can see and what he cannot. The frameworks remain robust; the applications occasionally reflect class position.

The Progress Studies framing has been criticized for under-attending to whom progress serves and at what cost. The criticism is legitimate. The seat does not require ignoring it; it requires holding the *productive* part of the framework (institutional velocity is real, capability formation is engineerable, the historical record on extraordinary performance is recoverable) while accepting that the *distributional* questions are not fully answered by the framework alone.
